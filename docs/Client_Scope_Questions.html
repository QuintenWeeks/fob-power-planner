Questions and recommended COAs for CSM(R) Hal Reynolds

Each question has a recommended COA, the position to walk in with, and an alternate with what would trigger it. Propose the position, then let him change it. This is easier for a client than a blank question.

The project: a hybrid design and dispatch app that minimizes cost for a FOB of 200 kW peak or less, using generators, solar and batteries. It approximates the Scioletti et al. (2017) model. Deliverables are the working app, a one-page spec sheet and the measured approximation error.

Dates: scope meeting Thu 5 Nov 0745 · check-in Thu 19 Nov 0745 · final presentation Thu 10 Dec 0845-0940.

The recommended scope if he has no strong views:

  • User: an S4 or base mayor planning a new base of under 150 people.
  • Horizon and fuel: one year at $50 per gallon, both editable.
  • Reliability: all demand met every hour, 30% load margin, and a spare generator rule that is on by default.
  • Solar: capped at a 100 m by 100 m site.
  • Output: equipment list, cost, gallons and convoys, compared with generators only, plus an hourly schedule and plain rules.
  • Delivery: one offline HTML file running HiGHS in the browser, as in Project 1.

These are recommendations for your team to confirm, not agreed positions. Values credited to the paper or prompt come from those documents. Anything marked "our assumption" still needs a source or his sign-off.

He shouldn't have to answer these. Take them to COL Scioletti or research them yourselves.

For COL Scioletti
  • How should approximation error be measured: against the full nonlinear model on which instances, and what size of gap is acceptable?
  • Which solvers and languages are allowed, and does the app need to solve live or can it use precomputed results?
  • What are the AI authorization level and the documentation requirement for this project?
  • How much of the role-play is fixed: does CSM(R) Reynolds set the requirements, or can the prompt overrule him?
  • Does the prompt's 200 kW cap apply before or after the load margin? At the paper's 30% margin, 200 kW of demand means sizing for 260 kW.
For the team to research
  • Generator fuel curves and minimum and maximum power ratings.
  • Battery voltage, capacity, efficiency and lifetime parameters.
  • Panel output, temperature and dust effects, and site irradiance data.
  • Published fully burdened fuel estimates (Noblis 2010 and Vavrin 2010 are in the prompt's references). Bring a range for him to react to in question 13.
